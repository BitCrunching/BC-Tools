New to equalizers?

Did you know that every 6 dB roughly doubles the strength of a band? Cqualy lets you shape the sound with simple sliders and see the wave change as you go. No experience needed: these short guides explain what each control does and how to fix common sound problems.

1. How an Equalizer Works

Sound is a mix of low, middle and high pitches. An equalizer splits it into bands, each tied to one frequency in hertz (Hz), and lets you turn each band up or down. Low numbers are bass. High numbers are treble.

RangeWhat you hearTypical use
20–120 HzRumble, weightKick drums, bass guitar
120–500 HzWarmth, boxinessBody of voices and guitars
500 Hz–4 kHzPresence, claritySpeech, lead instruments
4–20 kHzAir, sparkle, hissCymbals, breath, detail

dB or %. Each slider moves a band from -12 to +12 dB. A band at 0 dB is untouched. Click any dB value to see the same setting as a percentage of the original loudness.

SettingAs a percentage
-12 dB25%
-6 dB50%
0 dB100%, unchanged
+6 dB200%
+12 dBAbout 400%

Cut before you boost. Lowering the bands you do not want usually sounds cleaner than raising the ones you do. Small moves of 2 to 4 dB are often enough.

2. Picking a Preset, and When to Go Custom

A preset is a ready-made curve. It moves every band at once, and you can keep adjusting afterwards. As soon as you move a slider the preset box shows Custom.

PresetBest for
VoicePodcasts, calls, interviews
Bass boostSmall speakers, earbuds, thin recordings
Treble boostDull or muffled recordings
Rock, Pop, Electronic, Hip-hopMusic with strong bass and bright highs
Jazz, Classical, AcousticNatural, lightly shaped music
LoudnessQuiet listening, where bass and highs fade
Reduce rumble, Reduce hissWind, handling noise and tape hiss

How many bands. The band button switches between 7, 11, 15 and 31 bands. Fewer bands are quick for broad tone changes. 31 bands, one third of an octave apiece, are for precise fixes. Your preset or curve is carried over when you switch.

Trust your ears. Presets are starting points, not rules. Play the file while you adjust. The waveform updates as you move each slider.

3. Fixing Common Sound Problems

Most sound problems live in a narrow range. Find the symptom, then lower the matching bands a little at a time.

ProblemWhat to try
Muddy, boxy voiceLower the bands around 200 to 400 Hz
Harsh, tiring soundLower the bands between 3 and 6 kHz
Thin, weak soundRaise the bands around 100 to 250 Hz
HissLower the bands above 8 kHz, or use Reduce hiss
Rumble or wind noiseLower the bands below 60 Hz, or use Reduce rumble
Speech hard to understandRaise the bands around 1 to 3 kHz

Do not stack boosts. Raising many bands makes the whole file louder and can distort it. If you boost, lower the Volume slider to match. Cqualy also scales the saved file down if a boost would clip.

Noise is not removed. An equalizer turns frequencies down, it does not erase them. Cutting hiss also dulls the sound next to it.

4. Volume, Balance, Speed, Trim and Channels

Volume. The vertical slider next to the wave raises or lowers the whole file by up to 12 dB. Click its label to reset it.

Balance. Moves the sound toward the left or right speaker. Center leaves it as recorded. A mono file becomes stereo when you pan it.

Speed. From 50% to 200%. Speed works like a tape: faster is higher in pitch and shorter, slower is lower and longer.

Trim. Drag the two handles at the edges of the wave. Playback stays inside the selection, and Download saves only that part. Click the Trim label to get the full length back.

Channels. Mono, Stereo, 5.1 or 7.1 for the saved file. 5.1 and 7.1 are saved as WAV, and the sound goes to the front speakers. Cqualy does not create surround sound from a stereo file.

5. MP3 or WAV, and Using Video Files

FormatBest forLimit
MP3Small files that play everywhereLoses a little quality. Mono or stereo only.
WAVEditing, archiving, 5.1 and 7.1Large files, about ten times an MP3.

Pick MP3 for sharing. Cqualy saves MP3 at a high 320 kbps, so the difference from the original is hard to hear. Choose WAV when you will keep editing the file.

Video files. Drop an MP4, M4V, WebM, MOV or OGV video and Cqualy takes its sound. The download is audio only, not a video with new sound. A video with no sound track cannot be loaded. For MKV, AVI or FLV, convert the video to MP4 with Convido first.

Your files stay with you. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded. Continue where you left off keeps your last file and settings on this device only, and clicking the red cross clears them.
