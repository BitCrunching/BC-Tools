1. What EXIF Metadata Actually Reveals About You

Every photo carries a hidden block of data called EXIF. It records when, where and how the photo was taken.

Hidden dataWhat it showsPrivacy weight
GPS locationThe place the photo was taken, often within a few metersHighest
TimestampThe exact date and time, down to the secondHigh
Device make and modelThe camera or phone usedSome
Camera settingsAperture, shutter speed, ISO, focal lengthLow

It adds up. One photo seems harmless. Several photos together can map the places you visit often: home, work, the gym, a child's school.

What Cleanly does. It strips this data before a photo leaves your device. The picture looks exactly the same.

2. How to Strip Metadata Before Selling or Posting Photos

Photos that go to strangers are the ones where hidden data matters most.

Selling online. Listing photos are usually taken at home. Left alone, they can hand a buyer your address before you have even met.

Posting online. Many platforms strip metadata on upload, but not all. Downloaded or reshared copies can keep whatever survived.

Make it a habit. Run photos through Cleanly before you upload them anywhere public. It takes seconds and works on a whole batch.

iPhone photos (HEIC). Cleanly cannot read inside HEIC, so it always flags them. When you clean one, it becomes a PNG or JPG, as you choose on its row.

What it does not change. Anything you can see in the picture, like a street sign or a house number. Check that with your own eyes.

3. Cleanly for Photographers: Privacy Without Losing Quality

Camera settings are useful on your own copies. Location has no place in a file you deliver or publish.

FormatWhat Cleanly does
PNGLossless. Same pixels.
JPG and WEBPRe-saved at 95%. Looks the same at normal size, but not byte for byte.
HEICConverted to PNG or JPG, as you choose.
ColorsThe color profile is not carried over, so copies use standard sRGB.

Keep your masters. Keep the original with full metadata for your own use. Clean a JPG once, from the master, not again and again.

Clean last. Make it the final step before you export or deliver, for the whole batch at once.

Protect clients too. The location in a client's photo is often their home or a private place.

4. When Metadata Removal Matters Most

Not every photo carries the same risk. Start with these.

PhotoPriorityWhy
Taken at home or at workHighestIt maps to a place you spend regular time.
Of childrenVery highIt can show where a child is at set times.
Going to a strangerHighMarketplace buyers and dating matches have no trust with you yet.
Sent privately to people you trustLowerThere is less exposure.

A good default. Stripping costs no quality and little effort. Do it for anything that leaves your trusted circle.

5. Common Privacy Mistakes People Make With Photos

Assuming the platform handles it. Many do, but not all, and reshared or downloaded copies skip it.

Checking only what you can see. A photo can look safe and still carry your exact location.

Selling with untouched photos. Sellers shoot at home and upload as they are. It is the easiest way to expose an address.

Forgetting old posts. Stripping only protects photos you clean before sharing. Replace old posts with cleaned copies where the platform allows.

Cleaning only the sensitive ones. Judging each photo is how one gets missed. Make it a routine.

6. Cleanly for SVGs: Stripping Editor Metadata, Not EXIF

SVG files have no EXIF, because they are text that describes shapes. Design tools still add hidden data of their own.

What Cleanly findsWhere it comes from
An embedded metadata block, sometimes with author and license infoIllustrator, Inkscape, Sketch, Figma
Editor tags such as sodipodi:Inkscape, most often
XML commentsThe export process

Why it matters. None of it shows when the SVG draws, but it travels with the file to anyone you send it to.

You see it first. Cleanly lists what it found before it strips anything.

Nothing is lost. It removes only what the browser never drew. The shapes and colors stay, and you can still edit the file in a vector editor.
